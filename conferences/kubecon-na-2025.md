## Submission Constraints

### Character/Word Limits
- **Title:** Use title case, no explicit limit stated (keep concise)
- **Abstract/Description:** No explicit character limit stated; must be detailed, third-person, full sentences

### Speaker Limits
- **Max 3 proposals** per individual across all formats
- **Max 1 panel participation** per event
- **Max 1 non-panel session** accepted per speaker
- Panelists must represent **multiple companies**
- **Sessions with 3+ speakers identifying as men are auto-rejected**

### Content Restrictions
- No "low-effort, AI-generated proposals" (called "slop") — repeat offenders face bans
- No sales/marketing pitches
- No unlicensed or closed-source technology focus
- Cannot duplicate talks from Linux Foundation events within past year
- Case studies must explain differences if previously presented elsewhere

### Review Process
- Blind review: **No** (speaker identity visible)
- Program Committee reviews by track
- Co-chairs make final decisions

2. **Application Development** — Development practices, developer experience, frameworks
3. **Cloud Native Experience** — Community, business value, networking, adoption stories
4. **Cloud Native Novice** — Foundational concepts for newcomers
5. **Connectivity** — Networking, edge, telco, service mesh, ingress
6. **Data Processing + Storage** — Data systems, databases, volumes, stateful workloads
7. **Emerging + Advanced** — Research, HPC, cutting-edge concepts
8. **Observability** — Metrics, logging, tracing, alerting, OpenTelemetry
9. **Operations + Performance** — Autoscaling, reliability, optimization, SRE
10. **Platform Engineering** — Platform building, infrastructure automation, developer platforms
11. **Security** — Threat modeling, compliance, supply chain, runtime security

## What Reviewers Look For (Score High)

- **Clear problem identification** — What problem are you solving?
- **Contribution to cloud native ecosystem** — How does this help the community?
- **Technical depth** with big-picture perspective
- **Real-world implementation** — Not just theory
- **Benefit to attendees** — What do they gain?
- **Speaker proficiency** — Evidence of presentation ability (video links help)
- **Diversity and inclusion** — Diverse speaker lineups preferred
- **Open source angle** — CNCF projects, community tools

## Red Flags (Score Low / Reject)

- AI-generated "slop" — lazy, low-effort proposals
- Vendor/product-specific pitches
- Panels lacking company diversity
- Duplicate presentations from past year
- Unlicensed/proprietary technology focus
- All-male speaker panels (3+ speakers)
- Generic proposals not tailored to cloud native audience
- Missing the "three questions" (see Tips)

## Tips & Best Practices

### The Three Questions (address all three)
1. **What do you want to share?** — Your specific contribution
2. **What will attendees gain?** — Concrete takeaway
3. **How does this benefit the ecosystem?** — Beyond your company

### Writing Tips
- Write descriptions in **third person**
- Use **full sentences**, error-free
- Make it a detailed but focused pitch
- This description appears on the website if accepted
- Provide enough info for attendees to decide if the session is for them
- Use **title case** for titles

### Case Studies
- Welcome and valued
- Must show real-world implementation
- If presented elsewhere, explain what's different/new

### First-Time Speakers
- Explicitly encouraged
- Contact cfp@cncf.io for support
- Include any speaking experience, even informal

### Video Links
- Strongly encouraged
- Helps committee assess presentation style
- Can be informal recordings, meetup talks, etc.
